Where do these entries come from?

This snapshot includes all 105 profiles in NOAA’s threatened-and-endangered directory, plus separately researched whale shark and African penguin entries. It is not a complete list of every marine animal worldwide. NOAA also covers river-linked fish and two river-only dolphins; those dolphins are labeled.

NOAA entries show protection under U.S. law, including the exact groups covered. IUCN entries show worldwide risk. These are different systems. Some animals are doing well worldwide but still have groups that need protection.

Threats come from the linked animal source. “What helps” and “You can help” explain general responses to those threats, not a guaranteed cure. Sources checked November 21, 2026. Check the original page for later changes.

The problem, in a few words

Ocean animals need safer homes.

Plastic, harmful fishing and warmer seas put ocean animals in danger. Protecting their homes and changing a few habits can help.

Power use and ocean life

How energy affects animals.

The way we make power can affect both the sea and the rivers that lead to it.

Fuel, heat and seawater

Burning coal, oil or gas to make electricity releases gases that trap heat and warm the ocean. The sea also takes in some carbon dioxide, making the water more acidic. That makes it harder for corals and some shellfish to build their hard outer parts.

What helps: waste less power and support cleaner energy.

Water power and blocked fish

Hydro dams make electricity from moving water. But they can block fish, such as salmon, travelling between rivers and the sea. Dams can change water flow, and their spinning machines can hurt fish.

What helps: safe routes around dams, screens to keep fish away from machines, and river flows that support fish. The right fix depends on the river and the fish.

Learn more: NOAA: carbon dioxide and seawater · NOAA: helping fish pass dams

Ocean terms explained

Endangered means a species faces a very high risk of extinction in the wild. Bycatch means animals caught accidentally while fishing for something else. Conservation means keeping wild animals and their homes safe.

How you can help

Start at any age: reuse a bottle, share what you learned, or join a supervised cleanup. Families and communities can also support habitat protection and policies that make fishing safer.

About conservation status

An animal may be doing well worldwide but still be in danger in some places. Green turtles moved to Least Concern worldwide in 2025. They still need protection. The checklist tracks your actions, not the number of animals saved.
